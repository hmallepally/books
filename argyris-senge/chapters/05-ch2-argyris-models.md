# Chapter 2: Governing Values in Action

> *"The hardest gap to bridge is not the one between you and your competitor; it is the one between what you say you believe and how you actually behave."*

---

## The Illusion of Congruence

If you ask any executive to describe their management style, they will likely give you a list of admirable values: "I believe in open communication, empowerment, collaboration, data-driven decisions, and psychological safety."

This is their *espoused theory*—the set of beliefs, values, and justifications they claim guide their behavior (Argyris & Schön, 1974).

However, if you record their actual meetings and transcribe their words, a different pattern emerges. You see them interrupt subordinates, withhold critical information, design meetings to avoid disagreement, and make decisions based on personal intuition while demanding data from others.

This is their *theory-in-use*—the implicit cognitive rules and assumptions that actually govern their actions.

Argyris's research demonstrated a stark reality: while people's espoused theories vary widely, almost all individuals share the same theory-in-use, which Argyris termed **Model I** (Argyris, 1990).

## Model I: The Defensive Mindset

Model I is the default operational program for most human beings in organizational life. It is designed to avoid vulnerability, embarrassment, and loss of control. It is governed by four core values:

| Governing Value | Operational Behavior | Resulting Climate |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Unilateral Control** | Manage the environment and others unilaterally. Keep yourself in charge. | Defensive relationships, low trust. |
| **2. Maximize Winning** | Design interactions so that you win and others lose. Avoid losing at all costs. | Internal competition, political maneuvering. |
| **3. Suppress Negative Feelings** | Keep emotions out of the room. Do not show vulnerability or admit mistakes. | Artificial harmony, hidden resentments. |
| **4. Behave Rationally** | Frame all decisions as objective and logical. Dismiss others' concerns as emotional. | Intellectual dishonesty, bypassed realities. |

When leaders operate under Model I, they engage in *defensive reasoning*. They treat their own assumptions as facts, design tests that only confirm their beliefs, and attribute defensive motives to others while viewing themselves as objective.

The result of Model I is a self-fulfilling loop of learning paralysis. Because negative feelings are suppressed and unilateral control is maintained, real issues are bypassed. Because issues are bypassed, errors remain uncorrected. The organization becomes rigid, and double-loop learning becomes impossible.

## Model II: The Productive Mindset

To break free from this defensive loop, Argyris proposed **Model II**. Model II is not a soft, passive, or purely polite style of management. It is a rigorous, inquiry-based approach to leadership. It is governed by three cooperative values:

1. **Valid Information**: Sharing all relevant data, including uncomfortable truths, uncertainties, and personal biases. Ensuring that others have access to the same facts.
2. **Free and Informed Choice**: Designing interactions so that participants can make decisions based on valid information, rather than being coerced, manipulated, or nudged.
3. **Internal Commitment**: Fostering a climate where individuals feel personally responsible for the choices made, because they were actively involved in analyzing the data and making the decision.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      THE BEHAVIORAL GAPS                        │
│                                                                 │
│       ESPOUSED THEORY                    THEORY-IN-USE          │
│   (What We Say We Value)             (How We Actually Act)      │
│                                                                 │
│   • "I want honest feedback"   ───►   Reacts with defensiveness │
│   • "We value collaboration"   ───►   Decides unilaterally      │
│   • "Failure is a teacher"     ───►   Punishes mistakes         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The gap illustrated above is the primary source of organizational cynicism. When employees see leaders claim Model II values (espoused theory) while acting on Model I values (theory-in-use), they lose trust and retreat into their own defensive routines.

## Productive vs. Defensive Reasoning

Moving from Model I to Model II requires a shift in how we reason.

* **Defensive Reasoning**: Private, untested, and designed to protect the self. The reasoner assumes they are correct, collects only confirming data, and attributes negative motives to those who disagree.
* **Productive Reasoning**: Public, transparent, and open to refutation. The reasoner state their assumptions clearly, shares the data that led to those assumptions, and actively invites others to test and disprove their logic.

Productive reasoning is the engine of double-loop learning. It transforms disagreements from political battles into scientific inquiries.

> ⭐ **STAR Moment: The Cost of Politeness**
>
> *Many organizations suffer not from too much conflict, but from a pathological lack of productive conflict. In the name of politeness and teamwork, leaders withhold critical feedback and cover up errors. This polite compliance is a classic Model I tactic: it protects the speaker from the discomfort of confrontation, while leaving the organization's errors uncorrected.*
