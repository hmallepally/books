# Chapter 4: Action Science in Practice

> *"The ultimate test of Action Science is not whether you can explain it in a classroom, but whether you can map and redesign your own conversations under pressure."*

---

## The Left-Hand Column

Imagine you are having a conversation with one of your direct reports, Mark. Mark has missed his deadline for a critical market analysis report. You are frustrated, but you want to be a supportive manager.

The conversation goes like this:

**You**: "Hi Mark, how is that market analysis report coming along?"  
**Mark**: "It's taking a bit longer than I thought, but I'm making progress."  
**You**: "Okay, no problem. Just make sure it's thorough. We need it to be solid."  
**Mark**: "Don't worry, it will be."

On the surface, this is a normal, polite, collaborative conversation. But what were you *actually* thinking during this exchange?

If you were like most managers operating under Model I, your internal monologue looked more like this:

* *Why is he always late? He clearly didn't prioritize this.*
* *He says he's making progress, but I bet he hasn't even started the competitor section.*
* *If this report is late, the CEO will blame me. I don't trust him to get this done.*
* *I can't tell him how angry I am because he'll get defensive, and that will slow things down even more.*

This exercise—writing down what was actually said in the right-hand column and what you were thinking but didn't say in the left-hand column—is the primary diagnostic tool of **Action Science** (Argyris, 1990). It is called the **Two-Column Case Study**.

## The Two-Column Case Study Method

The two-column case study is a powerful self-help methodology designed to expose the gap between your espoused theory and your theory-in-use. It consists of four steps:

### 1. Select a Difficult Interaction
Choose a past conversation that did not go well, or a future conversation you are dreading. It should be an interaction where you felt frustrated, stuck, or unsuccessful.

### 2. Write the Context
Write a brief paragraph describing the situation. Who is involved? What was the goal of the conversation? What was the outcome?

### 3. Reconstruct the Transcript (Right Column)
Write a dialogue of what was actually said. Use direct quotes as close as you can remember. Do not summarize; write it as a script.

### 4. Record the Unspoken Thoughts (Left Column)
In the left column, write down what you were thinking and feeling but chose *not* to say at each point in the conversation.

## Analyzing the Transcript

Once you have written your transcript, analyze it using the following questions:

* **What did you do with your left column?** Did you suppress it entirely to maintain a superficial harmony? Did you let it leak out in sarcastic comments or body language?
* **What Model I values did you practice?** Did you try to maintain unilateral control by keeping the conversation vague? Did you try to "win" by silently judging Mark without testing your assumptions?
* **How did your behavior contribute to the problem?** By not sharing your left column, you deprived Mark of valid information. You did not test your assumption that he was lazy or unorganized, meaning you could not correct your own errors.

## Redesigning the Conversation

The goal of Action Science is not to blurting out your raw left column without filter. That is a Model I "fight" response. 

Instead, the goal is to **redesign the conversation** by translating your left-hand column into productive, Model II advocacy and inquiry:

* *Unspoken Thought (Left Column)*: "He is always late. He clearly didn't prioritize this."
* *Model II Redesign (Right Column)*: "Mark, this is the third time this month the report has been delayed (Data). I'm concerned that we aren't aligned on the priority of this project, and I'm starting to worry that our team's deadlines will slip (Assumption). Can you help me understand what is causing the delay? Are there resource issues we need to address?"

By bringing the underlying thoughts to the surface in a way that is open to inquiry and testing, you move from defensive bypassing to productive error-correction.

---

> ⭐ **STAR Moment: The Left-Hand Leak**
>
> *You cannot hide your left-hand column. When you suppress your real concerns to "keep the peace," they leak out. They manifest as coldness, micromanagement, sighing, or a lack of engagement. Your peers and reports will sense the discrepancy between your polite words and your hostile energy. The only way to prevent the leak is to bring the contents of the left column into the open productively.*
